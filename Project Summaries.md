# Noodle Service design

## **1\. What question did we begin with?**

We began with a simple yet powerful question:

**What is an unsaid human need that, when addressed, can create meaningful impact?**

Instead of assuming answers, we chose to listen.

---

## **2\. How did we explore this unsaid need?**

We used participatory and observational methods:

* Placed a **Wish Box** in public spaces for anonymous responses

* Created an **interactive wall** with a thought-provoking question

* Conducted **informal interviews**

* Facilitated **brainstorming sessions**

* Observed emerging **patterns**

From these interactions, **six strong needs** surfaced.

---

## **3\. How did we evaluate these needs?**

We applied the **5W Method**:

* **Who** is affected?

* **What** exactly is the need?

* **Why** does it matter?

* **When** does it surface most strongly?

* **Where** does it occur?

Through this critical evaluation, one need stood out — urgent, universal, and deeply human:

**The Need to Become Decisive**

---

## **4\. Why is decisiveness such an important need?**

Despite technological and intellectual evolution, humans continue to struggle with:

* Ambiguity

* Overthinking

* Emotional conflict

* Fear of consequences

Decisions often involve tension between:

* Morality vs practicality

* Career vs passion

* Stability vs risk

* Logic vs emotion

Indecision doesn’t just delay outcomes.  
 It creates **anxiety, self-doubt, and emotional exhaustion.**

---

## **5\. What was the core challenge?**

Decision-making is vast.

How do you design a solution that works across:

* Career choices

* Relationship decisions

* Financial risks

* Identity shifts

We needed to find the structure beneath all decisions.

---

## **6\. What pattern did we discover?**

By analysing successful and failed decisions, we observed:

Every decision silently interacts with:

* **Past** – What do I already have?

* **Present** – What is influencing me right now?

* **Future** – What could happen next?

This led us to identify three consistent anchors in strong decisions.

---

## **7\. What is the Triangle of Clarity?**

We distilled our insight into a framework called:

### **The Triangle of Clarity**

Every strong decision rests on three anchors:

1. **Drivers** – What is pushing or pulling me toward this choice?

2. **Information** – What do I know? What am I assuming?

3. **Consequences** – What are the best and worst outcomes?

We translated this into a guided, conversational Q\&A tool to help individuals navigate clarity.

---

## **8\. Did the framework work?**

Partially.

When tested, participants found it insightful, but too broad.

It lacked situational depth.

This failure revealed a key insight:

If confusion is contextual, clarity must be contextual too.

---

## **9\. Where does uncertainty feel most intense?**

We asked:

Where does decision anxiety peak?

One space stood out:

### **Final-year college students.**

Students who are:

* Dreaming of startups

* Considering corporate jobs

* Torn between passion and security

* Balancing ambition and fear

This wasn’t just a career decision.  
 It was an **identity decision.**

---

## **10\. What solution emerged?**

We redesigned the framework specifically for this scenario.

We made it:

* Sharper

* Relatable

* Guided

* Context-specific

We named it:

# **Noodle**

Because a confused mind feels like tangled noodles, twisted and overlapping.

Noodle doesn’t tell students what to choose.  
 It helps them untangle their thoughts so they can choose for themselves.

---

## **11\. What impact are we aiming for?**

Decisiveness is not about speed.

It is about **conscious clarity**.

By moving from:

* A broad human need  
   → to a structured clarity framework  
   → to a contextual, tailored solution

We transformed confusion into a navigable process.

Because real impact doesn’t always come from giving answers.

Sometimes, it comes from helping people ask better questions.

# Shell Research & SD hub

# **SHELL RESEARCH & SERVICE DESIGN HUB**

---

## **1\. What sparked the creation of the Shell Research & Service Design Hub?**

The initiative originated from an internal **Design Maturity Survey** conducted by Shell’s Experience Design (EXD) team.

The findings revealed a clear opportunity to strengthen:

* User Experience Research (UXR)

* Service Design (SD)

* Organisational understanding of design value

The survey highlighted systemic gaps affecting consistency, credibility, and strategic impact.

---

## **2\. What was the vision behind the initiative?**

The vision was to enhance the **effectiveness, consistency, and strategic influence** of UXR and Service Design across Shell.

Rather than solving isolated issues, the goal was to address systemic barriers preventing design from scaling as a mature organisational capability.

---

## **3\. How did you approach the discovery phase?**

To frame the problem space, we explored:

* What constitutes design maturity?

* How do leading organisations institutionalise UXR and Service Design?

* What governance models and operating structures enable scalable design practices?

Simultaneously, we conducted in-depth interviews with experienced Service Designers and User Researchers across business units to understand ground realities.

---

## **4\. What were the key research objectives?**

We aimed to understand:

* Practitioner backgrounds and team structures

* Common methodologies and frameworks

* Best practices and learnings

* Recurring challenges and organisational barriers

* Tools, systems, and documentation practices

This helped uncover both visible and unarticulated friction points.

---

## **5\. What key insights emerged from the research?**

The challenges identified were systemic and interconnected rather than isolated issues. Core friction points included:

* Limited understanding of design value at senior leadership levels

* Socio-political filters leading to insight pushback

* Business teams framing problems without research alignment

* Project silos and lack of cross-functional visibility

* Inconsistent documentation and systematisation

* Perception of EXD as “The Auditor” and instances of “UX Theatre”

* Difficulty in measuring and communicating impact

These issues collectively weakened credibility and influence.

---

## **6\. What were the root causes behind these challenges?**

Through systems mapping and dependency analysis, two foundational root causes emerged:

1. Lack of shared understanding of design value

2. Lack of systematisation and institutional memory

These root causes created ripple effects across governance, collaboration, and impact measurement.

---

## **7\. What strategic intervention was identified?**

After evaluating feasibility and scalability, the most impactful solution identified was:

### **Creation of a Centralised, Contextualised Playbook & Knowledge Library**

A dedicated platform tailored specifically to Shell’s ecosystem.

---

## **8\. What would this playbook include?**

The proposed Hub would embed:

* Shell-specific case studies

* Proven UXR and Service Design frameworks

* Templates and documentation standards

* Best practices and learning artefacts

* Governance guidelines

* Impact storytelling examples

This ensured relevance and contextual credibility.

---

## **9\. What problems was the solution designed to solve?**

The Hub aimed to:

* Institutionalise best practices

* Improve research rigour and consistency

* Increase leadership literacy around design value

* Reduce duplication of effort

* Enable knowledge retention and smoother handovers

* Strengthen EXD’s credibility

---

## **10\. What is the Shell Research & Service Design Hub?**

The outcome was the **Shell Research & Service Design Hub**, designed to function as:

* A playbook

* A capability-building repository

* A knowledge management system

* A design maturity accelerator

It serves both practitioners and leadership stakeholders.

---

## **11\. How was the solution validated?**

A working prototype of the Hub was developed and tested with practitioners.

Feedback highlighted:

* Strong practical utility

* High contextual relevance

* Improved potential for cross-team alignment

Constructive inputs on navigation, UI clarity, and information architecture were incorporated into refinements.

---

## **12\. What is the long-term impact potential?**

The Hub repositions EXD from being perceived as an execution layer to a **strategic enabler**.

By improving systematisation and strengthening the articulation of design value, the Hub contributes to:

* Long-term design maturity growth

* Stronger organisational alignment

* Greater credibility and influence of UXR & Service Design

---

## **13\. What is the core transformation achieved?**

The project moved from diagnosing maturity gaps to building a scalable organisational infrastructure.

Instead of addressing symptoms, it targeted foundational levers —  
 **design literacy and systematisation** — enabling sustainable impact.

# Wedlink

## **1\. What problem does this project explore?**

India’s wedding industry is a **billion-dollar ecosystem**, known for glamour and grandeur.

But behind the celebration lies an invisible workforce —  
 over **3 million people** who power the industry.

This includes:

* Decor teams

* Caterers

* Photographers & assistants

* Makeup artists

* Light & sound technicians

* Drivers

* Loaders & setup crews

* Freelancers & gig workers

The industry is not just about luxury.  
 It is a **livelihood engine**.

---

## **2\. What is the structural reality of this industry?**

The Indian wedding industry is:

* Highly seasonal

* Project-based

* Deadline-driven

* Informally structured

Teams are assembled per event.  
 When the season ends, so does the work.

During off-season months, workers:

* Return to hometowns

* Take daily wage labour

* Shift to unrelated odd jobs

* Survive without income continuity

This instability creates **fear**.  
 And fear creates **power imbalance**.

---

## **3\. Who are the most vulnerable in this ecosystem?**

The most vulnerable are support workers:

* Assistants

* Helpers

* Loaders

* Junior technicians

They often:

* Do not negotiate rates

* Have no written contracts

* Experience delayed payments

* Lack formal grievance systems

Their dependency makes exploitation easier.

---

## **4\. How was this issue researched?**

Through contextual inquiry across multiple domains of the wedding ecosystem, including:

* Informal interviews

* Field immersion

* Conversations with workers and vendors

The goal was to understand lived realities — not assumptions.

---

## **5\. What key insights emerged from the field research?**

### **1\. Income Instability & Delayed Payments**

* Payments frequently delayed

* No written agreements

* Earnings dependent entirely on season

* No digital income records

Financial unpredictability is normalised.

---

### **2\. Lack of Autonomy, Respect & Dignity**

* Workers seen as replaceable

* Long hours without acknowledgment

* No voice in decisions

* No safe way to question unfair treatment

Delivery is prioritised over dignity.

---

### **3\. Basic Human Needs Are Overlooked**

* No proper rest areas

* Unstructured meal arrangements

* Lack of safety measures

* No insurance or emergency support

Worker welfare is treated as optional.

---

### **4\. Informality Is Structural — Not Accidental**

This is not individual misconduct.  
 It is systemic.

* No contracts

* No payroll structures

* No formal hiring systems

* No dispute redressal mechanisms

The system runs on trust, relationships, and fear.

---

### **5\. No Social Safety Net**

Most workers lack:

* Provident Fund (PF)

* Insurance

* Savings buffers

* Documented work history

When season ends, stability ends.

---

## **6\. What intervention was proposed?**

To address these systemic gaps, a digital platform was conceptualised:

# **Wedlink**

A structured, accountability-driven gig platform for the wedding workforce.

---

## **7\. What does Wedlink do?**

### **1\. Job Discovery Platform**

* Vendors post verified job listings

* Workers apply transparently

* Hiring becomes structured, not relationship-dependent

---

### **2\. Escrow-Based Payment Assurance**

* Vendors deposit payment in advance

* Funds are securely held

* Payment released upon work completion

This ensures timely payouts and reduces financial exploitation.

---

### **3\. Digital Work History**

Workers build:

* Verified job records

* Ratings & reviews

* Skill tags

* Experience documentation

This increases credibility and bargaining power.

---

### **4\. Dispute Resolution & Helpline**

A structured support system for:

* Payment disputes

* Work condition complaints

* Harassment reporting

Support shifts from informal negotiation to institutional protection.

---

## **8\. What systemic shift does Wedlink aim to create?**

Wedlink aims to move the industry:

| From | To |
| ----- | ----- |
| Informal hiring | Structured gig marketplace |
| Verbal agreements | Escrow-backed contracts |
| Invisible labour | Recognised professionals |
| Fear-driven loyalty | Platform-based trust |
| Exploitation | Accountability |

---

## **9\. What is the larger impact vision?**

Wedlink is not just a hiring platform.

It is an attempt to:

* Bring dignity to invisible labour

* Create income continuity

* Introduce financial transparency

* Formalise an informal ecosystem

* Build accountability without removing flexibility

The goal is to strengthen the **invisible spine** of India’s wedding economy —  
 by giving workers visibility, structure, and security.

# Piracy

### **What was this project about?**

Understanding digital piracy as a human behavior shaped by platform design, social norms, and access to content.

### **What was the core research focus?**

To explore why users engage in piracy, how they justify it, and how online environments influence this behavior.

### **What research methods were used?**

Netnography, auto-ethnography, and interviews to study digital communities, peer-to-peer networks, and content-sharing platforms.

### **What key problem was identified?**

Piracy is often normalized and rationalized due to anonymity, ease of sharing, community culture, and perceived inaccessibility or high cost of content.

### **What behavioral insights emerged?**

Users frequently view piracy as “harmless access” rather than theft, creating a moral disconnect between consumption and the impact on creators.

### **What design opportunity was identified?**

To intervene at the platform level by redesigning cues, feedback systems, and awareness mechanisms that influence ethical decision-making.

### **What solutions were proposed?**

Interface nudges, watermarking, verification systems, reporting tools, and transparency mechanisms to make consequences more visible and reduce opportunistic piracy.

### **What is the intended impact?**

To shift user perception and behavior from normalized piracy toward responsible digital consumption through design-led, systemic interventions rather than purely punitive measures.

# Human behaviour

### **What was this project about?**

Understanding why street food vendors do not consistently practice clean hand hygiene despite being aware of health risks.

### **What was the core research focus?**

To study real-world hygiene behaviors in street food ecosystems and identify what influences vendors’ everyday actions.

### **What research methods were used?**

Field observations, interviews, and secondary research to examine behavioral patterns, contextual constraints, and social norms.

### **What key problem was identified?**

Hygiene lapses are not caused by ignorance alone — they are driven by time pressure, customer demand, economic priorities, lack of infrastructure, and normalized unsafe routines.

### **What behavioral insights emerged?**

Vendors prioritize speed and income over sanitation, while customers often overlook hygiene for taste and convenience — reinforcing existing habits.

### **What opportunity did the research uncover?**

To design interventions that make hygienic behavior easier, more visible, and socially reinforced rather than effortful or punitive.

### **What was the proposed direction?**

Develop low-cost, scalable, workflow-friendly solutions that integrate seamlessly into vendors’ daily routines.

### **What is the intended impact?**

To enable sustainable behavior change in street food ecosystems by aligning hygiene practices with vendors’ practical and economic realities.
